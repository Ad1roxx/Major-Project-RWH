| `least_discriminating` | lulc |
| `sd_composite` | 0.1242 |
| `discrimination_total_pct` | 100.0 |

**Caption**

Two decompositions of the same composite, each criterion shown twice so the pair can be read
together. LEVEL is the mean over in-district cells of w_i*r_i / V, the share of the score's
level a criterion supplies (Napolitano & Fabbri 1996). VARIATION is the Pratt index: with V
an exact linear combination, the standardised coefficient of V on r_i is beta_i =
w_i*sd(r_i)/sd(V), and beta_i*corr(r_i, V) partitions var(V), summing to 100%. (a) gives the
three shares absolutely, (b) the two derived ones as a ratio to the assigned weight, so 1.0
marks a criterion taking exactly the share it was given. TWO CAVEATS on the variance
measure. It partitions the variance of the CONTINUOUS baseline score, not membership of the
High class, which depends on the upper tail and on where the breakpoints fall - which is why
Fig. 17, measuring class share, ranks the criteria differently. And it is defined only for a
linear aggregator, so it is computed on AHP + WLC; the TOPSIS closeness coefficient is not a
weighted sum. Land cover is the least discriminating per unit of assigned weight at 0.08x,
about five times below slope, while also taking 1.32x its share of the level.

texture at r = 0.45 (rho = 0.44); those two criteria carry 0.58 of the total weight between
them, so the overlap is worth stating - at r = 0.45 they share about 20% of their variance
and the model is to that extent counting one signal twice, but they are far from redundant.
Two separate reasons to read rho with care, which should not be conflated. TIES: soil puts
78% of cells on one score and land cover 66%, both being categorical lookups, so a rank
statistic is breaking large blocks of equal values. CONCENTRATION: slope is not tied - its
largest exact repeat is 0.1% of cells - but it is squeezed into the top of its range, with
86.0% of cells above 0.90, which is the defect Fig. 2 documents the ramp only partly curing.
Either one pulls rho and r apart, and slope against land cover is where they diverge most:
rho = 0.34 against r = 0.06.
