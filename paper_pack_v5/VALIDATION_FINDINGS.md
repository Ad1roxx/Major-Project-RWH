# Validation against MGNREGA water works — findings and how to write them

Source of every number: `validation/VALIDATION_NUMBERS.txt`, generated by
`tools/validate_mgnrega.py` (2026-10-20). If this file and that one disagree,
**that one wins**. Figures: `fig_val_roc`, `fig_val_scores`, `fig_val_gain`,
`fig_val_map`.

---

## 1. What was tested, in one paragraph

The suitability surfaces were compared with **3,119 MGNREGA works** recorded
under the two water-related workcode categories (WC, WH) in Chandrapur. They
are accepted geotags from NRSC's Bhuvan Bhugram portal, both phases, thinned
to one per 3 × 3-cell neighbourhood (filter ledger in §3). The works were
compared against **10,000 random background cells** drawn uniformly over the
whole district (seed 42). That was done for all **20 surfaces**: 4 methods ×
5 scenarios. Metrics:

- ROC AUC, with a 2,000-resample bootstrap 95% CI;
- paired bootstrap differences and the DeLong test;
- precision-recall AUC and the continuous Boyce index;
- a gain table (each class's share of works against its share of area).

Robustness checks: spatial thinning, settlement removal, single-criterion
AUCs, a within-cropland test, photo-confirmed subsets, and a permutation
negative control.

**This is not an accuracy assessment.** It measures agreement with where
MGNREGA water works were actually built.

## 2. Results

### 2.1 Headline (baseline scenario)

| Method | ROC AUC [95% CI] | PR AUC (prevalence 0.238) | Boyce |
|---|---|---|---|
| AHP + WLC | **0.661** [0.650, 0.672] | 0.360 | 0.836 |
| AHP + TOPSIS | 0.629 [0.618, 0.640] | 0.331 | 0.873 |
| Fuzzy-AHP + WLC | 0.663 [0.652, 0.674] | 0.362 | 0.837 |
| Fuzzy-AHP + TOPSIS | 0.631 [0.620, 0.642] | 0.334 | 0.881 |

- **All 20 surfaces** score AUC 0.629–0.715. Every 95% CI excludes 0.5.
- **Negative control:** shuffling the baseline map's cell values gives an AUC
  of **0.496**, so the pipeline behaves correctly.

### 2.2 Gain table: the planner's number (baseline AHP + WLC)

| Class | % of district area | % of water works | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| High | 6.4 | 12.4 | **1.93×** |
| Medium | 52.5 | 66.3 | 1.26× |
| Low | 41.1 | 21.3 | **0.52×** |

High land holds about twice its share of the works, and Low land about half.
All four methods agree: High 1.84–1.95×, Low 0.52–0.62×. The sensitivity at
the paper's High cutoff (> 0.85) is 9.2–12.4% across methods.

### 2.3 Method comparison (baseline, paired)

- **Aggregation matters:**
  - WLC beats TOPSIS by **+0.030 to +0.034 AUC**. The 95% CIs exclude 0
    and DeLong p < 10⁻¹⁰⁰.
  - **Within cropland the gap disappears.** All four methods score
    0.670 there (to three decimals). So TOPSIS loses its agreement on
    *non-cropland* cells, not on farmland.
- **Weighting method doesn't matter:** AHP against Fuzzy-AHP differs by
  **0.002**.
  - The difference is "significant" (DeLong p ≈ 10⁻⁶⁷) only because the two
    surfaces are almost identical, which makes the test extremely precise.
  - Report it as **negligible in size**, not as a meaningful difference.

### 2.4 Scenarios: the paper's thesis shows up again

- **AUC by scenario, AHP + WLC:**
  - baseline 0.661;
  - community-focused 0.668;
  - maximise recharge 0.691;
  - low-earthwork 0.691;
  - fertile farmland **0.710**.
- **Comparing the decisions:**
  - Changing the **weighting judgements** moves AUC by **0.049** (0.661 to
    0.710).
  - Changing the **aggregation rule** moves it by about 0.03.
  - Changing the **weighting method** moves it by 0.002.
- Analyst decisions move agreement with practice more than the choice of
  algorithm does. That is the same ordering the paper finds for class shares
  (Fig. 12).
- **Caution, and it must be in the text:** the farmland scenario agrees best
  partly because MGNREGA works sit on cropland (87.8% of works against 61.1%
  of background cells) and that scenario raises the land-cover weight to
  0.229. Higher AUC here means *closer to where MGNREGA built*, **not**
  *better for rainwater harvesting*. Do not use it to rank the scenarios.

### 2.5 Robustness

- **Spatial thinning** (one work per 1 km cell, 2,563 kept): AUC 0.654
  [0.641, 0.666] for AHP + WLC, essentially unchanged. The result isn't
  driven by clusters of works.
- **Settlement confound:**
  - Works sit slightly *farther* from settlement cells than background (median
    6.12 km against 5.81 km; Mann-Whitney p = 0.001), not closer.
  - Removing the settlement criterion lowers AUC by only 0.001–0.004.
  - The agreement **is not** an artefact of works being built near villages.
- **Land-cover confound:**
  - Land cover alone gives AUC 0.644, the best single criterion.
  - The full model gives 0.661–0.663.
  - **Within cropland only** the model still scores **0.670**, where land
    cover can't contribute, so the other five criteria carry real
    information.
- **Single criteria:** land cover 0.644, rainfall 0.606, slope 0.578,
  drainage 0.563, soil 0.557, settlement 0.539. The combined model beats every
  one of them, though only modestly beats land cover.
- **Photo-confirmed structures** as the presence set:
  - strict (n = 12): AUC 0.684 [0.530, 0.815];
  - lenient (n = 42): AUC 0.696 [0.614, 0.771].

  Both point estimates sit above the noisy set's 0.661, as you'd expect if
  the non-structures dilute the signal, but the intervals are wide.
- **Boyce index** 0.84–0.88 (baseline). The ratio of observed to expected
  works rises with score. Its spread under randomness is wide (sd 0.26 in this
  implementation), so treat it as supporting the AUC, not standing alone.

### 2.6 The photo audit that shapes all of this

- **Design:** 240 blinded photos, 30 per category segment, two labellers.
- **Agreement:**
  - 75.8% raw;
  - Cohen's κ 0.459 on six labels;
  - κ 0.392 on structure-or-not.
- **What the water categories contain:**
  - **WC: 16.7%** strict / 50.0% lenient show a harvesting structure;
  - **WH: 16.7%** strict / 46.7% lenient.
- **No category passed** the protocol's inclusion rule under the primary
  (strict) reading.
- **So 50–83% of the presence points are not harvesting structures.** Every
  AUC above is diluted towards 0.5, and is best read as a **lower bound** on
  agreement with built structures.
- **Deviations from protocol,** to declare in Methods:
  - the protocol was completed after labelling began, before unblinding;
  - disagreements were resolved by a fixed strict rule, not a joint session;
  - labeller A's sheet was re-aligned by one row on the coordinator's
    instruction;
  - the key was opened while diagnosing that misalignment.

### 2.7 A second reference: CGWB's proposed recharge sites (disagrees)

- **The reference:** CGWB's 2021 aquifer mapping report proposes 418
  recharge structures (150 percolation tanks, 112 check dams, 156 recharge
  shafts), sited where the aquifer has room.
- **Result:** against the same background, the baseline scores them
  **AUC 0.362** [0.341, 0.382], below chance. 70.3% fall in Low land and
  1.0% in High.
- **Where our High land sits:** CGWB judges recharge infeasible (shallow,
  rising water table) in six north-eastern blocks that hold 43.1% of our
  High area and 13 of the top 25 sites.
- **Reading:** the model agrees with *where water works are built*, but not
  with *where hydrogeologists would recharge the aquifer*. Full
  interpretation and consequences for the paper's framing in
  `CGWB_FINDINGS.md`.

## 4. Deviations from the validation brief (declare them)

1. **No asset-type field exists,** so "farm pond / check dam / …" could not
   be selected. The presence set is the water category segments, with their
   measured precision (§2.6).
2. **No work-status field exists.** Only stages 0 and 3 were pulled, and
   both are completed or existing assets.
3. **Figures are PNG only,** per the project rule. No vector PDFs.
4. **Block boundaries on `fig_val_map` are drawn from the 15 Census 2011
   talukas,** which share names and extents with the 15 MGNREGA blocks. That
   is an approximation.

## 5. How this supports the paper, and what it cannot claim

**It supports:**
- **Independent field evidence agrees with the map.** Where people actually
  built water works under MGNREGA, the model scores higher than at random,
  for every method and scenario (AUC 0.63–0.72, all CIs above 0.5).
- **The map's classes mean something on the ground.** High land carries
  about twice its share of works, and Low about half.
- **The agreement survives the obvious confounds.** It holds under thinning,
  removing settlement, and restricting to cropland.
- **It reinforces the thesis.** Analyst decisions (weighting judgements) move
  agreement more than the algorithm does. Within the algorithms, aggregation
  matters and the weighting method doesn't, which matches Figs 11–12.
- **It closes the "no validation at all" gap,** with a reproducible,
  pre-registered-style protocol and honest deviations.

**It cannot claim:**
- **Accuracy:** "the model is validated" or "the map is X% accurate". The
  presence set is mostly non-structures, and the data are presence-only.
- **That High sites are good sites.** People build where they own land and
  have budget, not only where the site suits harvesting. Agreement with
  practice is not agreement with an ideal.
- **That one scenario is better than another for harvesting.** The farmland
  scenario's higher AUC partly reflects the cropland bias of the works
  (§2.4).
- **Strong discrimination.** AUC ≈ 0.66 is conventionally "poor to fair".
  Say *moderate* and explain the dilution.

**Suggested Abstract sentence:**
> Against 3,119 MGNREGA water works, the baseline surface ranked recorded
> works above random district cells (AUC 0.661, 95% CI 0.650–0.672), and
> High-class land held 1.9 times its area share of works; because a blinded
> photo audit found only 17–50% of works in the water categories to be
> harvesting structures, these figures are a lower bound on agreement with
> built structures, not a measure of accuracy.

**Where it goes:** a new Results subsection, "Agreement with recorded water
works". Its Methods go after the sensitivity analyses. The old "no accuracy
metric is reported" lines in the Abstract and Conclusion must be replaced by
the wording above, still without the word "accuracy".
