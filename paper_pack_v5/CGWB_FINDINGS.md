# What CGWB says about Chandrapur's groundwater, and what it means for the paper

**Source:** Nelofar, Nanda & Venktesham (2021), *Aquifer Mapping and Management
of Ground Water Resources, Chandrapur District, Maharashtra*. Central Ground
Water Board (NAQUIM). Facts and page references are in
`data/raw/cgwb/PROVENANCE.md`. The comparison numbers are generated by
`tools/validate_mgnrega.py` and appear in `validation/VALIDATION_NUMBERS.txt`.

**Read this before writing the recharge justification.** It changes it.

---

## 1. The finding in brief

1. **The district is not groundwater-stressed overall.**
   - It is categorised **Safe**, with a stage of groundwater development of
     **19.35%** (2017 estimation). CGWB itself lists "low development of
     ground water resources" as an issue.
   - **The newest assessment (GSDA/CGWB, Maharashtra 2023) keeps all 15
     blocks Safe**, with extraction from 17.7% (Ballarpur) to 44.6%
     (Chimur). That puts the district at **29.3%**, computed from the 15
     block rows (`supporting/gsda/PROVENANCE.md`).
   - Extraction is rising, from 16.3% (2004) to 19.3% (2017) to 29.3%
     (2023), though methods differ between cycles. That rise is the one
     honest pressure argument available. It is still far from the 70%
     Safe threshold.
2. **Decline is real but local.**
   - Pre-monsoon levels fell over 52% of the district (2011–20), mostly
     slowly.
   - A **significant** fall (> 0.20 m/yr) covers only **7%**, in isolated
     parts of Bhadravati, Chandrapur, Warora, Chimur and Pombhurna.
   - Levels **rose** over 47%.
3. **CGWB plans recharge only where the aquifer has room.**
   - Artificial recharge is judged feasible in **9 blocks** (2,614 km²).
   - It is judged **not feasible in Jiwati, Mul, Nagbhir, Pombhurna, Saoli
     and Sindewahi**, where the post-monsoon water table is shallow (< 3 m)
     and rising.
4. **Our High zone sits disproportionately in those six blocks.** They cover
   31.2% of the district but hold **43.1%** of the baseline High area. **13 of
   the top 25 sites** are in them.
5. **CGWB's 418 proposed recharge structures fall mostly in our Low zone.**
   - **70.3%** of the sites fall in Low land, which is 41.1% of the area
     (1.71×), and only **1.0%** in High (0.15×).
   - Against random cells, our baseline scores them **below chance**: AUC
     **0.362** [0.341, 0.382]. It's the same for each type: check dams 0.364,
     percolation tanks 0.386, recharge shafts 0.337.

## 2. Why the two disagree

They answer different questions.
- **Our model** (rainfall-led: rainfall 0.43) asks *where does a lot of rain
  fall on gentle, permeable, farmed land with drainage nearby?* That favours
  the wetter north-east.
- **CGWB** asks *where does the aquifer have empty space to take extra
  water?*, i.e. a deep unsaturated zone and falling levels. That favours the
  drier west and south (Warora, Chandrapur, Rajura, Bhadravati, Korpana). In
  the wet north-east the ground is already full after the monsoon.

The model has no layer that sees depth to water or aquifer storage. That is
exactly the "did you use groundwater recharge-zone data?" question the
reviewer asked.

At the same time, the MGNREGA water works **agree** with the model (AUC
0.661). They cluster in the north-east too: Chimur 435, Nagbhir 351 and
Brahmapuri 342 retained works. So **where water works are actually built**
matches the model, while **where CGWB says recharge is hydrogeologically
useful** does not.

## 3. What this means for the paper

**Don't claim:**
- that Chandrapur's groundwater is declining or stressed district-wide (CGWB
  says Safe at 19%);
- that the High zones are the best places for **groundwater recharge**.

**The objective framing has to soften.** The reviewer's "conflict" point is
stronger than we thought. Two honest options, and the team must choose:

- **(A) Reframe as surface rainwater-harvesting suitability.** Present the
  model as identifying where runoff can be **captured**: where rain is
  plentiful, slopes gentle, soils and land cover suitable, and demand
  near. The surface criteria do this. Recharge becomes one *use* of
  harvested water, valid where the aquifer has room. That is checked
  site by site, and CGWB's map shows where. The MGNREGA agreement supports
  this framing directly.
- **(B) Keep "recharge-oriented"** and report the CGWB comparison as a major
  limitation: the model lacks an aquifer-capacity criterion, and its High
  zone overlaps the blocks where CGWB judges recharge infeasible.

**Recommendation: (A) plus a full report of the comparison.** It changes no
model result, only the claim. It also answers the reviewer more honestly,
and turns the CGWB comparison into a finding: surface suitability and
aquifer capacity point to different parts of the district, so siting needs
both. Under (A), the soil scoring's preference for permeable soils needs one
sentence. It still favours infiltration *at the structure*, which reduces
standing-water losses, but it should no longer be sold as maximising
aquifer recharge.

**Either way, add to the paper:**
- **Discussion:** a paragraph with the four numbers:
  - the district is Safe (19.35% in 2017; 29.3% in 2023);
  - 43.1% of High lies in non-feasible blocks;
  - 70.3% of CGWB sites fall in Low;
  - AUC 0.362.
- **Limitations:**
  - no depth-to-water or aquifer-storage layer;
  - **the soil ordering favours permeable soils (Loam best).** That suits
    recharge structures, but storage structures (farm ponds, tanks) would
    prefer clay. A storage-oriented variant would reverse the ordering, which
    is future work. (A test of this exists, but **the authors have decided
    not to report it in this draft.**)
- **Future work:** combine the two maps to recommend **structure type**:

  | Your map | CGWB | Recommend |
  |---|---|---|
  | High | recharge feasible (deep water table, room in the aquifer) | Recharge structures (percolation tanks, check dams, shafts) |
  | High | recharge not feasible (shallow, rising water table, e.g. the north-east) | Storage structures (farm ponds, village tanks) for dry-season use |
  | Low | recharge feasible | Fewer, targeted recharge structures, as CGWB plans |

  The surface map says where runoff is worth capturing; CGWB's says what to
  do with it. That's why the combination matters under framing (A).

## 3b. How CGWB chose its sites (confirmed from the report), and why the paper should not try to match them

**Documented in the report** (Chapter 7, Tables 7.1–7.2, and the block
chapters):
1. **Feasibility filter:** artificial recharge only where the post-monsoon
   water level is **deeper than 3 m bgl**, excluding shallow levels with a
   rising trend. That comes from about 100 CGWB and GSDA monitoring wells,
   with 2011–20 trends. Result: 2,613.95 km² in 9 blocks; 6 blocks excluded.
2. **Aquifer space:** feasible area × unsaturated thickness below 3 m ×
   specific yield (about 0.03) = 4,032.39 MCM.
3. **Water available:** surplus (non-committed) monsoon runoff per
   watershed = 42.58 MCM. **This, not aquifer space, limits the plan.**
4. **Counts:** surplus water ÷ unit capacity (percolation tank 0.20 MCM,
   check dam 0.03 MCM, recharge shaft 0.06 MCM) = 150 + 112 + 156
   structures, split by block. Expected recharge 32.04 MCM/yr at 75%
   efficiency.
5. **Placement:** the report calls the coordinates "tentative locations"
   and **does not state how individual points were placed.** NAQUIM
   practice is usually expert placement on drainage lines from toposheets,
   but this report does not say so. Don't write it as fact.

**For the paper:**
- **Describe CGWB's sites accurately:** a water-budget allocation within a
  depth-to-water feasibility zone, placed by experts. Not a suitability
  model.
- **The model was not tuned to them, and shouldn't be.**
  - Reproducing the points would need data the project lacks: depth to
    water, specific yield, watershed surplus, and a fine stream network.
  - It still couldn't recover the unpublished placement step.
  - Fitting a model to a reference it is then compared with is circular.
- **The fair use is block-level:** the feasibility split (9 against 6
  blocks, Table 7.1) can label which High zones suit recharge and which suit
  storage, with no change to any score. Present it as a discussion point
  or future work.
