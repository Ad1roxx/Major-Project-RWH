## 1. The single biggest change: the soil legend was wrong (2 Oct 2026)

- **What was wrong.** The soil raster (ISRIC SoilGrids250m, `TEXMHT_M_sl1`,
  Hengl et al. 2017, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0169748) carried no legend in
  the project copy. The original download still had its `LEGEND` tag, and
  checking against it showed **three of the four classes present had been
  mislabelled**:

  | Code | Was read as | Old score | Actually | Share of district | Score now |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | Sand | 0.55 | **Clay** | 18.6% | 0.25 |
  | 4 | Silt Loam | **1.00** | **Clay Loam** | **77.8%** | 0.60 |
  | 6 | Sandy Clay Loam | 0.80 | Sandy Clay Loam ✓ | 3.2% | 0.80 |
  | 7 | Silt Clay Loam | 0.50 | **Loam** | 0.4% | **1.00** |

- **How the error arose.** The labels were assumed when `criteria.py` was
  written and wrongly attributed to a slide. We found the error ourselves by
  tracing provenance back to the original file.
- **Hydrology was affected too.** The hydrologic soil groups behind the
  SCS-CN runoff surfaces carried the same error and were remapped to the
  standard USDA-NRCS assignment.
- **Effect.** Almost every result in the old script changed (section 3).
  Present this as the strongest example of the project checking its own
  work, not as an embarrassment.
- **Soil scoring is now cited to two free sources:**
  - **USDA TR-55, Appendix A**, texture-based hydrologic soil groups: Loam in
    group B, Sandy Clay Loam in C, Clay Loam and Clay in D.
  - **FAO Training Manual No. 5, *Irrigation Methods*** (Brouwer, Prins, Kay
    and Heibloem, 1988), Annex 2, Table 7, basic infiltration rates: loam
    10–20, clay loam 5–10, clay 1–5 mm/h.

  Neither source prescribes score values. The scores themselves are a
  modelling judgement, which is tested in the Loam robustness check (section 3).

## 2. Other changes since the last review

**Analysis and statistics** (refinements, none of them overturns a result):
- **Class shares are area-weighted.** The grid is lat/long, so cells vary
  0.81% in area. One function, `class_share_pct`, now feeds every figure, and
  a test enforces it.
- **Effects are computed from unrounded shares.** They had been quantised to
  0.01 pp. Example: aggregation on High is −0.957, not −0.955.
- **"Effective weight" is now two measures:**
  - **level contribution**: the share of the score's level each criterion
    supplies;
  - **variance share** (Pratt index): the share of the variation across the
    district, which is what discriminates between sites.

  They disagree sharply (see the sensitivity results in section 3).
- **Baseline scenario renamed** from "Baseline (balanced)" to "Baseline
  (rainfall-led)". Rainfall's weight of 0.428 exceeds the bottom four
  criteria combined.
- **New analyses added:**
  - a factorial decomposition of the algorithm effects;
  - a ranking-versus-cutoff partition of the class changes;
  - a criterion correlation matrix;
  - percentile versus fixed-band rainfall scoring;
  - a Loam robustness check;
  - a test of the spatial structure of method disagreement.
- **Sensitivity wording:** the three analyses are now called
  "complementary", not "independent", because they share one criteria stack.

**Figures:**
- 17 publication figures are generated by code (PNG). They went through 14
  external review rounds, backed by automated checks for overlap, palette and
  rounding.
- Six figures still have to be made in QGIS or a drawing tool: study area,
  preprocessing chain, architecture, baseline map, hydrological surfaces and
  field workflow.

**Validation data:**
- **Source identified and pulled.** Bhuvan Bhugram MGNREGA accepted geotags
  for Chandrapur:
  - 82,289 records across all 15 blocks;
  - Phase I (works before Nov 2017, tagged retrospectively) and Phase II
    (tagged at completion);
  - 99.2% of points inside the district;
  - GPS accuracy at most 20 m (median 4 m).
- **Records carry only a work-category code**, not a structure type. Which
  codes count as harvesting structures is being decided by a **blinded
  two-labeller photo audit**:
  - 240 photos, 30 per category code;
  - the protocol is fixed and signed before labelling;
  - agreement is measured with Cohen's kappa.

  Only after that will an AUC be computed for each method.
- **Caveats to state:**
  - the data are presence-only;
  - people build where they have land and budget, not only where the site is
    suitable;
  - several geotags can belong to one work.
- **No accuracy figure exists yet.**
- **Privacy:** the observer names in the raw data are excluded from every
  output.

**Application (browser map):**
- **Data layer picker** shows, as colour-coded zoomable overlays with a
  legend each (like QGIS pseudocolour):
  - the six criteria, with rainfall as the 2014–2024 mean;
  - population density, plus a population heatmap (requested by a professor);
  - the suitability score;
  - Curve Number, runoff depth and runoff coefficient for a 100 mm storm.

  Zones, rivers and sites stay toggleable on top.
- **Raw values or model score:** raw values are shown by default, with a
  switch to the model's 0–1 score for that criterion.
- **Click-to-read:** clicking anywhere shows every layer's value and score
  for that ≈300 m cell.
- **Candidate sites per algorithm:** one overlay for each of the four
  algorithms, each in its own colour and ring size, toggleable separately.
  Hovering a site lists which other algorithms chose a site within 2 km.
- **Basemap** changed to Esri dark grey (keyless).
- **Automated checks:** 14/14 smoke tests pass (was 11).

## 6. Reviewer questions raised since the last review, with justifications

The team has decided **not to change the model** at this stage. These answers
strengthen the justification for what already exists.

### 6.1 "Rainwater harvesting and groundwater recharge are conflicting criteria"

**Justification:**
- **The objective is one goal.** The study does not optimise two competing
  goals. It targets **recharge-oriented rainwater harvesting**: capturing
  monsoon runoff so it reaches groundwater.
- **Water is lost to timing, not shortage.** Chandrapur receives about
  1,490 mm a year, concentrated in one short monsoon, and most of it runs
  off: the mean runoff coefficient for a 100 mm storm is 0.637.
- **So the target is retention in the subsurface.** That points to recharge
  structures (percolation tanks, check dams, recharge pits).
- **The criteria are consistent with that one objective:**
  - five of the six (rainfall, slope, drainage density, land cover,
    settlement proximity) favour the same locations for storage and
    recharge structures alike;
  - only soil texture differs in direction, and its scoring deliberately
    favours infiltration (Loam 1.00 > Sandy Clay Loam 0.80 > Clay Loam 0.60
    > Clay 0.25);
  - that order matches USDA TR-55 hydrologic soil groups and FAO
    infiltration rates.
- **The project's scenarios include "Maximise recharge"** (CR 0.0044), which
  pushes the recharge emphasis further.
- **Honest framing:** the difference between storage and recharge
  structures is real. The study made an explicit choice; it does not deny
  the difference. A separate storage-oriented surface (soil scoring
  reversed) is future work.
- **[Team to verify before citing:]** a CGWB source on Chandrapur's
  groundwater status, either the district groundwater brochure or the
  Dynamic Ground Water Resources block categories. Use it only if it
  supports declining or stressed groundwater.

### 6.2 "Did you use groundwater recharge or recharge-zone data?"

**Answer honestly: no dedicated hydrogeological layer was used.**

**Justification:**
- **Surface controls are included.** The six criteria are the factors most
  often used in groundwater-potential and recharge-zone studies (slope,
  soil, land cover, drainage density, rainfall), so recharge potential is
  represented through its surface controls.
- **Infiltration is quantified.** The SCS-CN hydrology layers (Curve
  Number, runoff depth and runoff coefficient for a 100 mm storm) estimate
  at each cell how much rain infiltrates rather than running off. These
  are visible on the app's map.
- **Why subsurface layers were left out.** Lithology, lineaments,
  geomorphology and aquifer maps were excluded so that every input is
  openly available, consistently gridded across the district and
  reproducible in other districts.
- **What the output is.** It screens surface suitability at district scale.
  Site-level hydrogeological checks remain part of normal pre-construction
  practice.
- **Possible future sources:** GSI Bhukosh (geology, lineaments,
  geomorphology), NRSC Bhuvan groundwater-prospect maps, CGWB/India-WRIS
  water levels, and CGWB NAQUIM aquifer maps.

### 6.3 "How standardised are the resolutions? Could they be more precise?"

**Justification:**
- **One grid for everything.** All inputs are resampled to one ~300 m grid
  (450 × 422 cells, 123,555 in the district), so every criterion is compared
  cell for cell.
- **Resampling matches the data type:** bilinear for continuous layers,
  nearest-neighbour for categorical ones.
- **The grid's correctness is checked:** the district area matches the
  official figure to 99.7%.
- **The grid size is a deliberate middle ground.** It sits between the
  finest inputs (SoilGrids 250 m, HydroSHEDS ~90 m) and the coarsest (CHIRPS
  rainfall at ~5.3 km). A finer grid would not add real information to the
  coarse layers; it would only imply precision the data cannot support.
- **Rainfall varies smoothly at district scale**, and CHIRPS is the finest
  openly available, long-term (2014–2024) gridded rainfall record.
- **SoilGrids** is the finest openly available soil texture raster.
- **Do not claim it "couldn't be more precise".** Finer open products do
  exist for some layers, and naming them as future refinement is the honest
  position:
  - land cover (ESA WorldCover, 10 m, against MODIS 463 m now);
  - elevation (30 m DEMs);
  - population (WorldPop 100 m).
- **The resolution suits the purpose:** identifying priority zones for
  field survey, not designing individual structures.

### 6.4 "How do you validate why you used this approach?"

- **Robustness evidence that needs no ground truth:**
  - three complementary sensitivity analyses;
  - agreement across four MCDA algorithms (15 sites chosen by all four;
    528.5 km² High under all four);
  - a Loam robustness check.
- **Ground truth is in progress.** The MGNREGA geotag dataset and the
  blinded photo-audit protocol are in place. AUC per method will be
  computed after labelling.
- **Until then, say exactly this:** "We verified the software, not yet the
  map."
